Typosquatting, also called URL hijacking or domain squatting, is registering a domain that looks like a real one but contains a small mistake: a swapped letter, a missing character, a different top-level domain, or a lookalike character. People who mistype the address, or who do not look closely at a link, end up on the attacker's site.

## How attackers use lookalike domains

The attacker registers several close variants of the target's domain. Some point the domain at a copy of the real login page. Others use it to send email that appears to come from the company or one of its suppliers, which is a common start to invoice fraud and business email compromise.

A fake login page on a lookalike domain collects passwords and payment details. Email from `acme-corp.com` instead of `acmecorp.com` is easy to miss, especially on a phone. The domain can also host malware downloads, and customers defrauded through a lookalike often blame the real company.

## What to do about it

- Keep monitoring, so you hear about new lookalike registrations before they are used.
- Register the most likely misspellings and other top-level domains of the client's domain to take them off the market.
- Report malicious lookalikes to the registrar or hosting provider. Trademark owners can also file a UDRP complaint to take over the domain.
- Set up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC on the real domain. They do not stop a lookalike domain, but they make the real one harder to spoof. Mail filtering that flags lookalike sender domains covers some of the rest.
- Show staff what lookalike domains look like and ask them to report anything suspicious. Bookmarks for frequently used sites cut down on typing mistakes.
