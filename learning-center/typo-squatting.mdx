Typosquatting, also called URL hijacking or domain squatting, is registering a domain that looks like a real one but contains a small mistake: a swapped letter, a missing character, a different top-level domain, or a lookalike character. People who mistype the address, or who do not look closely at a link, end up on the attacker's site.

## How attackers use lookalike domains

The attacker registers several close variants of the target's domain. Some point the domain at a copy of the real login page. Others use it to send email that appears to come from the company or one of its suppliers, which is a common start to invoice fraud and business email compromise.

The main risks are:

- **Phishing.** A fake login page on a lookalike domain collects passwords and payment details.
- **Business email compromise.** Email from `acme-corp.com` instead of `acmecorp.com` is easy to miss, especially on a phone.
- **Malware.** The domain hosts downloads or links that install malware.
- **Brand damage.** Customers who are defrauded through a lookalike domain often blame the real company.

## What to do about it

- **Monitor.** Keep watching for new lookalike registrations so you hear about them before they are used.
- **Register the obvious variants.** Buying the most likely misspellings and other top-level domains of the client's domain takes them off the market.
- **Report and take down.** Report malicious lookalikes to the registrar or hosting provider. Trademark owners can also use legal routes, such as a UDRP complaint, to take over the domain.
- **Protect email.** SPF, DKIM, and DMARC on the real domain do not stop a lookalike domain, but they make it harder to spoof the real one. Mail filtering that flags lookalike sender domains helps with the rest.
- **Train users.** Show staff what lookalike domains look like and ask them to report anything suspicious. Bookmarks for frequently used sites reduce typing mistakes.
