SSL/TLS is the encryption that protects traffic between a browser and a website. A misconfiguration is any mistake in how it is set up: an expired or untrusted certificate, a site that still answers over plain HTTP, or missing security headers that tell browsers how to protect the connection. Each one weakens the protection SSL/TLS is supposed to give.

## Why it matters

- **Interception.** Without properly configured SSL/TLS, anyone on the network path, for example on public Wi-Fi, can read logins, session tokens, and form data.
- **Tampering.** If HTTPS is not enforced, or HSTS is missing, an attacker can downgrade a connection and change the page before it reaches the browser: injecting scripts, swapping payment details, or redirecting to a fake site.
- **Compliance.** GDPR, HIPAA, and PCI DSS all expect sensitive data to be encrypted in transit. A misconfigured customer-facing site can put a client out of compliance.
- **Trust.** Browser certificate warnings drive visitors away and make the site look compromised even when it is not.

Email encryption is checked separately: Telivy's mail authentication checks look for mail servers that do not support STARTTLS.

## How Telivy rates severity

### High

- **Certificate not found or no issuer.** The site has no valid certificate, or the certificate has no identifiable issuer, so a secure connection cannot be established or trusted. This leaves it open to man-in-the-middle attacks.
- **Revoked certificate.** The certificate authority has revoked the certificate and it should no longer be trusted.
- **HTTPS not enforced.** The site does not redirect HTTP to HTTPS, so connections can be made, or downgraded, without encryption.

### Medium

- **Expired or expiring certificate.** The certificate has expired or expires within 30 days.
- **Self-signed certificate.** Browsers show security warnings for self-signed certificates on public sites.
- **Weak signing algorithm.** The certificate is signed with a hash weaker than SHA-256, which makes forgery easier.
- **Missing Content Security Policy (CSP).** Without a CSP header, the site lacks a key defense against cross-site scripting and data injection.
- **Missing or misconfigured HSTS.** A missing, short-lived, or wrongly scoped Strict-Transport-Security header leaves the site open to protocol downgrade and cookie hijacking.

### Low

- **Overly broad CSP.** A Content Security Policy built on wildcards gives much weaker protection than a tightly scoped one, even though a policy exists.
- **Excessive certificate validity.** The certificate is valid for longer than the 398-day industry maximum, which extends the exposure window if the key is ever compromised.

## How attackers exploit it

Attackers use these gaps for man-in-the-middle attacks, downgrade attacks that force a weaker protocol, session hijacking with stolen cookies, content injection, and phishing sites that copy a real one. The results are data breaches, fraud, regulatory penalties, and lost customer trust.

## Remediation

- Renew certificates before they expire and automate renewal where possible. [SSL/TLS certificates](/products/external-assessments/unsecured-emails/ssl-tls-certificates) covers certificate findings in Telivy.
- Replace self-signed, revoked, or weakly signed certificates with ones from a trusted certificate authority.
- Redirect all HTTP traffic to HTTPS and add an HSTS header with a long `max-age`.
- Add a Content Security Policy and tighten wildcard directives over time.
- Rescan after each change to confirm the finding is gone.
