---
title: Find Email Services Without SSL/TLS Encryption
sidebarTitle: Overview
description: 'How Telivy detects mail servers that accept unencrypted POP3, IMAP, or SMTP connections, and what to do about ports 110, 143, and 25.'
---

Email sent or collected without TLS (or its predecessor, SSL) crosses the network in plain
text. Anyone positioned to intercept the traffic can read the messages and the passwords
used to fetch them, much like reading a postcard.

Telivy finds this by checking which mail ports on the company's servers accept unencrypted
connections. The results are reported as **Email without SSL/TLS Found** in the Network
Security section of both External Scans and Risk Assessments.

## Ports this finding can list

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Port 110 (POP3)">
    POP3 downloads email from a server.

    **Risk:** POP3 on port 110 sends everything, including the password, in plain text, so
    it can be read by anyone intercepting the traffic. POP3 over SSL (POP3S on port 995)
    encrypts the connection.

    **What to do:** Check whether anyone still uses POP3. If they do, move them to POP3S on
    port 995 and close port 110.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Port 143 (IMAP)">
    IMAP reads and manages email on a server.

    **Risk:** Like POP3, IMAP on port 143 sends data in plain text. IMAP over SSL (IMAPS on
    port 993) encrypts it. How much damage a leaked login could do depends on what the
    account can access.

    **What to do:** Check whether IMAP is in use and how sensitive the mailboxes are. Move
    clients to IMAPS on port 993 and close port 143.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Port 25 (SMTP)">
    SMTP sends email between servers, and port 25 has to be open for anyone on the internet
    to deliver mail to the company.

    **Risk:** A badly configured SMTP server can be abused by spammers to relay mail, and
    weak authentication lets outsiders send mail through it.

    **What to do:** Decide whether the company needs to run its own SMTP server. If it does,
    require encryption with STARTTLS, enforce authentication (for example SASL), and block
    open relaying.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
