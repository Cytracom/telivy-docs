An SSL certificate, now more accurately called a TLS certificate, proves that a website is
who it claims to be and lets browsers encrypt traffic to it. Certificates are issued by
certificate authorities (CAs), which check that the requester controls the domain before
issuing one.

## What a certificate does

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Authentication">
    The certificate proves the website's identity, much like an ID card. Visitors rely on
    this before they type in a password or a card number.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Encryption">
    The certificate lets the browser and the website set up an encrypted connection, so
    anyone intercepting the traffic can't read or change it.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

Think of a letter sent in a locked box (encryption) with a verified seal on the address
label (authentication). A certificate does both jobs for web traffic.

## What Telivy checks

Telivy checks the certificates on the company's domains and subdomains and raises a
finding for each of these problems.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Expired certificates and certificates about to expire">
    An expired certificate makes browsers show a warning, and many visitors leave. It can
    also make it easier to intercept traffic with a man-in-the-middle attack. Automatic
    renewal helps, but someone still has to set it up and watch that it works.

    Telivy flags certificates that have expired or expire within 30 days.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Self-signed certificates">
    A self-signed certificate wasn't issued by a trusted CA, so browsers don't trust it and
    show a security warning. That costs visitors and trust.

    Telivy flags self-signed certificates as a Medium severity finding.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Weak signature algorithms">
    Certificates signed with MD5 or SHA-1 are open to collision attacks, which can let an
    attacker forge a certificate and impersonate the site. Weak algorithms can also fail
    compliance requirements.

    Telivy flags any certificate on the company's domains and subdomains that is signed
    with a weak algorithm.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Certificate issuer not found">
    Browsers trust a certificate through a chain: a root CA such as Let's Encrypt or DigiCert
    signs an intermediate certificate, which signs the website's certificate. If the issuer
    (usually the intermediate) can't be found, the chain breaks, the browser can't verify the
    certificate, and visitors see a warning.

    Telivy flags certificates whose issuer can't be found.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Certificate validity longer than standard">
    A long validity period means fewer renewals, but it also gives attackers more time to
    exploit a weakness in the algorithm or a leaked private key. Browsers increasingly
    distrust certificates that are valid for longer than the industry standard.

    Telivy flags certificates with a longer validity period than usual and shows the
    details in the finding.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
