The **AI Exposure Score** shows how much a client's staff use generative AI tools from company devices. Prompts can carry customer records, contracts, or source code off the network, so wide AI use is a data risk even when each tool is legitimate. The score is on the **AI Exposure Score** card on the assessment's **Overview** tab, and **View all websites** opens the full list on the **Web Activity** tab. See [Web activity and AI use](/products/risk-assessments/overview#web-activity-and-ai-use) for how sites are classified as AI tools.

## How the score is calculated

The score uses two measurements from the assessed devices:

- **Tool usage volume:** how many different AI tools are in use. Any AI tool at all starts the count, and more than two, then more than five, each raise the exposure further.
- **Adoption rate:** the share of users who use AI tools. Adoption above 5%, 25%, and 50% of users each raise the exposure.

Tool usage volume carries more weight than adoption rate. The result is scaled to 0 to 100, and a higher score means more AI exposure.

## Reading the score

| Score | Risk level | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 20 | Low (green) | Little AI exposure. |
| 21 to 50 | Moderate (yellow) | Some AI use that a policy or approved tool list can manage. |
| 51 to 80 | High (orange) | Widespread AI use that could expose company data. |
| 81 to 100 | Critical (red) | Heavy, unmanaged AI use that needs attention now. |

A high score is a reason to talk to the client about which AI tools are approved and what data may go into them. It does not mean a tool is malicious.
