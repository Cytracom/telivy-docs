## What the Risk Score is

The Risk Score is a number from 0 to 100 that summarizes a client's security posture. Higher is better. It is the main number on a Risk Assessment's overview, and the one clients usually ask about in a QBR.

Telivy calculates it with a **harmonic penalty model**, a formula that turns the severity and number of open findings into a score. Closing a few low-impact issues cannot inflate it much.

<Note>
  This page covers Risk Assessments, which combine agent-based endpoint scanning with Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace data. External Assessments are scored differently.
</Note>

## What you see in the portal

When you open a Risk Assessment, the main dashboard shows:

- A **Risk Score gauge** from 0 to 100, colored from red for low scores to green for high ones.
- The **risk band** (Critical, High, Medium, or Low) next to the gauge.
- A **sparkline** of recent scans, once the assessment has at least two completed scans. Until then it reads "Enable monitoring to track score history".
- **Top Actions**, the findings that would raise the score most if fixed.
- **CIS Controls Passing**, a separate percentage explained [below](#cis-controls-passing).

## Risk bands

Each score falls into a risk band. Reports, exports, and score history all use the band:

| Band | Score range | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Low Risk | 90 to 100 | Strong posture, minimal open findings |
| Medium Risk | 70 to 89 | Moderate posture, some areas need attention |
| High Risk | 50 to 69 | Significant gaps, prioritized remediation recommended |
| Critical Risk | 0 to 49 | Serious exposure, immediate action recommended |

With clients, lead with the band. "Your environment is rated Medium Risk" matches what they will see in the report.

## How the score is calculated

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="In plain terms">
    Every finding has a severity: Critical, High, Medium, or Low. Higher severities subtract more from the score. Telivy adds up the penalty for all open findings and converts the total into a 0 to 100 number.

    Critical findings hit the score much harder than low-severity ones, and fixing any finding, even a low-severity one, always improves it. Larger environments are measured against higher expectations, so a 200-device client with 10 open findings loses fewer points than a 10-device client with the same 10. Platforms you haven't connected, such as Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, are left out of the score entirely.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Technical detail">
    The Risk Score uses a **harmonic penalty model**:

    ```
    score = 100 / (1 + √totalPenalty)
    ```

    **Penalty accumulation:**
    Each finding contributes a penalty based on its severity weight:

    | Severity | Weight |
    |---|---|
    | Critical | 2.0 |
    | High | 0.15 |
    | Medium | 0.075 |
    | Low | 0.02 |

    **Environment normalization:**
    Each penalty contribution is normalized against `maxExpected = perEntityCap × entityCount`, where entity count is derived from the number of devices, users, domains, and web-facing hosts. Larger environments are not penalized just for having more assets.

    **Platform filtering:**
    Controls with a `platformRequirement` of `m365` or `gws` are filtered out if the corresponding integration is not connected. Filtered controls do not contribute to the score in either direction.

    **Monotonic improvement guarantee:**
    The penalty function uses `log(1 + rawCount/maxExpected)` with no hard caps. Every remediation reduces the penalty, which always increases the score. There is no threshold below which fixing a finding has no effect.

    The **CIS Controls Passing** percentage shown alongside the gauge is a separate calculation: `positiveBlocks / totalBlocks × 100`, where a block is considered passing if it has no open findings. It is not derived from the harmonic penalty model and is not the number displayed in the Risk Score gauge.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## CIS Controls Passing

Next to the Risk Score, the dashboard shows a **CIS Controls Passing** percentage. It is a separate metric.

| Metric | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Risk Score (0 to 100) | Severity-weighted penalty across all findings, normalized by environment size |
| CIS Controls Passing (%) | What proportion of applicable CIS security controls have no open findings |

A client can have a high Risk Score and a low CIS Controls Passing percentage when their open findings are spread across many low-severity controls. A single Critical finding can pull the Risk Score down while most CIS controls stay green.

Use CIS Controls Passing when the conversation is about compliance frameworks, and the Risk Score when it is about business risk.

## Top Actions

The **Top Actions** table ranks findings by how much fixing them would raise the Risk Score. It shows:

- Finding category and severity
- Number of affected assets
- **Score Gain**, the points the Risk Score would increase if this finding were fully remediated
- Action type (auto-fix available, requires configuration, manual remediation)

The first row is always the fix with the largest score gain. In a QBR, it tells the client where to start and how much the score should move.

## Score history

Once an assessment has at least **two completed scans**, score history shows up in two places:

- The **sparkline** on the Risk Score card, covering recent scans.
- **Risk Score Over Time**, a full chart on the **Risk** tab covering every scan.

Both need monitoring turned on for the assessment. Score history is kept per assessment, so you can show a client how the score has moved since remediation started.

## Why the score changes between scans

A scan can find issues that were not there before, such as a newly published CVE, a configuration change, or a new device, and those lower the score. Resolved findings stop counting and raise it.

The score can also move when the findings stay the same. Adding or removing devices, users, or domains changes the size baseline the penalty is measured against. Telivy also adjusts severity weights and normalization from time to time and announces those changes in the release notes.

## Risk Priority Score

The Risk Score describes the whole environment. The **Risk Priority Score (RPS)** answers a different question: which vulnerability to fix first. Every CVE finding, and every device with CVEs, gets an RPS from 0 to 100 that combines three factors:

| Factor | What it measures |
| --- | --- |
| Likelihood | How likely the vulnerability is to be exploited |
| Impact | What an attacker gains, based on CVSS severity and how critical the affected asset is |
| Exposure | How reachable the affected asset is |

The RPS falls into a priority band:

| RPS | Priority |
| --- | --- |
| 80 to 100 | Critical |
| 60 to 79 | High |
| 40 to 59 | Medium |
| 20 to 39 | Low |
| Below 20 | Informational |

RPS appears in the **RPS** column on the **Priorities** tab (every CVE and device across all severities, ranked by RPS), on the **Risk** tab, in the vulnerability tables under **Security**, and on the **Endpoints** table under **Inventory**. Where a row groups several findings, such as a device, the column shows the highest RPS in the group.

### Adjusting the RPS weights

The RPS is a weighted sum: `RPS = weight × Likelihood + weight × Impact + weight × Exposure`. To change how much each factor counts for a client, open the assessment, select **More**, and choose **Risk Scoring Weights**. Each weight must stay within its allowed range, and the three must add up to 1.00. **Save** stores them as an override for that company. The dialog shows where the current weights come from (**Company override**, **Agency default**, or **System default**), and **Reset to defaults** removes a company override.

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Why do two similar-looking environments have different scores?">
    Two environments that look alike can still differ in their findings, in which platforms are connected (Microsoft 365, Google Workspace), and in their number of assets. Size matters too: a 10-device client and a 200-device client with the same types of findings get different scores, because expectations scale with the environment.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="My score dropped but I didn't change anything. Why?">
    The most common cause is a newly published CVE in software that was already installed. New devices or users also change the size baseline, and scoring model updates can shift the number. Model updates are listed in the release notes.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does accepting or resolving a finding affect the score?">
    Yes. Accepted and resolved findings are left out of the score. Accepting a finding records a deliberate decision by you or the client to live with that risk.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How often is the score recalculated?">
    Every time a scan completes, whether you started it or monitoring did. The score always reflects the most recent scan.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What's the difference between Risk Score and CIS Controls Passing?">
    The Risk Score is weighted by severity and adjusted for environment size. CIS Controls Passing is the share of applicable controls with no open findings. They measure different things and can move independently. See [CIS Controls Passing](#cis-controls-passing).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Will the scoring model change over time?">
    Yes. Telivy updates severity weights, normalization, and control mappings from time to time, which can shift scores. Changes are announced in the release notes.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
